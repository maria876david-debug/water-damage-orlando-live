Water Damage: Call Insurance First or a Restoration Company First?

A homeowner on Reddit asked exactly this after finding a leaking drain and a soaked crawl space. The answer isn't one call before the other — it's a sequence: document, mitigate, then claim, all on day one. Here's how that order plays out in an Orlando home.

• Published: November 25, 2026

A homeowner on r/homeowners recently found water lines running from under a kitchen-sink cabinet and a big wet patch in the crawl space below, fed by a drain that had been leaking for a long time. The repair itself wasn't the hard part. The hard part was one question: who do you call first? Their own insurer, who'd send out their recommended company — or a restoration company they picked themselves? They worried the insurer's people would be biased toward denying the claim. They also worried that even calling the insurance company might count as a claim and push their rates up.

If you're standing in an Orlando kitchen looking at a swollen cabinet floor, that's the exact fork in the road you'll hit. Here's the order that protects both your home and your claim.

Quick Answer

Stop the water and take photos first. Then get a restoration company out for emergency mitigation, and open the insurance claim the same day. You don't have to choose one call before the other — but drying can't wait on an adjuster's calendar, and your policy expects you to limit further damage. The order that protects you is photos, mitigation, claim — all inside the first 24 hours.

Who should you call first after finding water damage?

Honestly? Neither call matters as much as the sequence around them. A restoration company stops the damage from growing. An insurer decides what gets paid for, based on the evidence in front of them. Those are two different jobs, and you need both.

Start with the water itself. Shut it off, get the standing water up, and document everything. Then call a restoration company for an assessment and emergency drying, and file the claim the same day — online, through your agent, or on the claims line.

The mistake we see is the pause. Homeowners wait three or four days for an adjuster before letting anyone touch the house, and by then a one-room drying job has crept under the flooring into the next room. The claim didn't get safer. The bill got bigger.

Will the insurer's recommended company work against you?

The worry in that Reddit thread isn't paranoid. Insurers run preferred-vendor programs, and companies on those lists get a steady flow of work from the carrier. Their scopes can run conservative, because the relationship they protect is the one with the insurer, not with you.

Here's what matters: in Florida, you are not required to use the company your insurer suggests. You can hire your own restoration company, and the insurer still has to evaluate your claim on its merits. What settles arguments isn't whose logo is on the truck. It's the documentation — timestamped photos, moisture readings, and a drying log that shows exactly what was wet and when it dried.

So don't accept a thin scope just because it came from the carrier's list. Get the damage measured properly, and keep copies of everything yourself.

Does calling your insurer automatically count as a claim?

Not every phone call is a claim. A claim opens when you report a specific loss and ask the insurer to pay for it. A general coverage question to your agent is a different thing — though calls to a claims hotline can be logged, and a logged inquiry about a specific leak can follow the policy.

That cuts both ways, and it's where the thread's second worry gets tricky. Florida homeowners already pay some of the highest premiums in the country, and a water claim on your history can affect renewal pricing. But sitting on a real loss is the worse move. Policies require prompt notice of a loss. Report a kitchen leak six weeks late, with mold behind the cabinets, and the insurer has a genuine argument that the extra damage is on you.

The practical line: if the damage is clearly bigger than your deductible, file it, file it promptly, and file it with documentation. If it's borderline, get the restoration assessment first — a measured scope tells you which side of the deductible you're actually on before you decide.

What should you do in the first hour, before either call?

  1. Shut the water off at the fixture valve under the sink, or at the main if you can't find the local shutoff.
  2. Cut power to the wet area if water is near outlets or appliances, and only if you can do it without standing in water.
  3. Photograph and film everything before cleanup — the leak point, the spread, the damaged cabinet and flooring. This is your claim evidence.
  4. Keep the failed part. A split supply line or a cracked drain fitting is proof the loss was a plumbing failure, not wear you ignored. Don't let anyone throw it away.
  5. Get standing water up with towels, a mop, or a wet vac, and move dry belongings out of the wet zone.
  6. Write down when you found it. Discovery time matters for sudden-versus-gradual arguments later.

Why does mitigation need to start before the adjuster visits?

Because your policy says so, in effect. Homeowners policies carry a duty to mitigate — you're expected to take reasonable steps to stop a loss from getting worse. An adjuster's visit might be days away. Wet drywall doesn't wait politely in the meantime.

In Central Florida's humidity, mold growth can begin within 24 to 48 hours on wet drywall and cabinet wood. If you let a kitchen sit wet for a week waiting for permission, the insurer can reasonably argue the mold remediation part of the bill was preventable — and decline that part.

Drying first doesn't hurt a claim when it's documented. Adjusters work from moisture readings, photos, and the mitigation invoice every day; a dried room with a proper paper trail is a normal file, not a suspicious one. What does the first 24 hours look like in an Orlando home?

Most Orlando homes sit on a concrete slab, so there's no crawl space to catch a leak the way the Reddit poster had — water spreads sideways instead, across tile or vinyl plank and into the base of the drywall. It travels further than it looks. A sink leak found at breakfast can have wet wall cavities two rooms over by the next morning.

A typical first day here runs like this: shutoff and photos in the first hour; extraction and drying equipment placed the same day; the claim opened before you go to bed. Over the next two to four days, a tech checks moisture readings daily and pulls equipment room by room as readings hit dry standard. The adjuster usually visits while drying is underway or just after, working from the same readings.

One Orlando-specific wrinkle: AC condensate lines cause a huge share of local water calls, and those claims get extra scrutiny because a clogged drain looks gradual. The discovery photos and the failed part matter even more on those. Our emergency water extraction crews handle the first-day work across Orlando and Orange County.

How much does emergency mitigation cost in Orlando?

Our published ranges for residential mitigation: a single wet room runs $1,200–$2,500; multi-room drying runs $2,800–$5,500; severe flooding or black water contamination runs $6,000–$12,000+. The full breakdown lives on our Orlando water damage restoration cost page.

Those numbers are also your deductible test. If an assessment puts your kitchen at the low end of the single-room range and your deductible sits close to it, paying directly can be the sensible route — no claim on your history. If the scope lands in the multi-room range, the claim usually makes sense. Either way, you can't run that test until someone has measured the damage. Guessing from the doorway is how people under-file and under-dry.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I start cleanup before my insurer approves it? Yes. Your policy expects you to limit further damage, so extract water and start drying right away. Protect yourself with timestamped photos, moisture readings, and a kept invoice — that's the paper trail an adjuster works from.

Do I have to use the restoration company my insurer recommends? No. In Florida you can hire your own restoration company and your insurer still has to evaluate the claim. Vendor-list companies are an option, not a requirement.

Will filing a water damage claim raise my premium in Florida? It can affect renewal pricing, which is why the deductible test matters. But reporting a genuine loss late can cost far more — insurers can dispute damage that grew while you waited. Ask your agent about your specific policy.

How fast does mold start after a kitchen leak in Florida? Growth can begin within 24 to 48 hours in warm, humid conditions, which is most of the year in Orlando. That short window is exactly why mitigation starts the same day, not after the adjuster's visit.
