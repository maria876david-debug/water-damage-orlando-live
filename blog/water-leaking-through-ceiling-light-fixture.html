Water Leaking Through a Ceiling Light Fixture

If water is leaking through a ceiling light fixture, do not touch the light switch, the fixture, or anything wet near it. Turn off power to that room at the breaker panel, keep everyone away from the dripping area, and call a professional.

Water dripping from a ceiling light is one of the most alarming leaks a homeowner can discover, and for good reason. A light fixture is wired directly into your home's electrical system, and water pooling around it can energize the fixture, the switch, and the wet ceiling around it. This is not a leak to investigate with your hands.

Why Is a Leaking Light Fixture So Dangerous?

A ceiling light fixture contains live wiring, and water is a conductor. When water reaches the fixture's electrical box, current can travel through the water, the fixture itself, and any wet surface touching it. You do not need to touch a bare wire to be shocked — a wet switch plate or a damp ceiling near the fixture can be enough.

There is also a fire risk. Water inside an electrical box can cause short circuits, and saturated ceiling materials can hide smoldering damage. If you notice flickering, a burning smell, or a breaker that keeps tripping in that room, treat it as an emergency and keep the power off until a professional has inspected it.

What Should I Do Right Now?

  1. Do not touch the switch, fixture, or wet ceiling. Do not try to turn the light off at the switch.
  2. Turn off the breaker. Go to your breaker panel and switch off the circuit for that room. If you are unsure which breaker it is, switch off the main breaker. Do this with dry hands while standing on a dry floor.
  3. Keep people and pets away from the dripping area and any puddle beneath it.
  4. Stop the water source if you can do so safely. If a bathroom or appliance is directly above, turn off its water supply. Do not climb into a wet attic or ceiling space.
  5. Catch the drip. Place a bucket under the leak from a safe distance, and move furniture and electronics out of the way.
  6. Call for help. You need both the leak source fixed and the ceiling dried, plus an electrical check before that fixture is used again.

What Should I NOT Do?

  • Do not flip the light switch to "test" whether it still works.
  • Do not unscrew or remove the fixture while it is wet.
  • Do not poke a hole in the ceiling to drain it while power is still on.
  • Do not use a hair dryer or heat gun on the wet fixture or ceiling.
  • Do not turn the breaker back on until the ceiling is dry and the wiring has been checked.

Where Is the Water Coming From?

Water rarely starts at the fixture. It travels along joists and pipes and drips out at the lowest convenient opening — often a light fixture or a ceiling vent. Common sources above a leaking fixture include a bathroom leak (toilet supply line, shower drain, or tub overflow), a leaking AC drain line or air handler in the attic, a roof leak, or a burst pipe in the ceiling cavity.

Finding the true source matters, because drying the ceiling without fixing the source just sets up the next leak. A moisture assessment traces the wet path through the ceiling cavity rather than guessing from the drip point.

What Happens After the Ceiling Dries?

Once the source is fixed and the ceiling is dried with structural drying equipment, the damaged section often needs repair. Wet drywall around a fixture may sag or lose strength and need a patch or replacement. The fixture's wiring and electrical box should be inspected before the light is used again — water-damaged wiring does not always look damaged from below.

If the ceiling shows signs of bowing or sagging, treat it seriously. A waterlogged ceiling can collapse without much warning. Our guide on signs a ceiling may collapse from a leak explains what to watch for. For active leaks, call emergency water extraction at (407) 557-9924 right away.
