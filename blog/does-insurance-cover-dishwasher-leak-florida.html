Does Insurance Cover a Dishwasher Leak in Florida?

It usually depends on whether the leak was sudden or gradual. A burst hose is generally the kind of sudden event policies cover; a slow drip left for months is often treated as gradual damage and excluded. Your own policy wording decides your case.

A dishwasher leak is one of the most common kitchen water damage events in Florida homes, and it is also one of the most disputed in insurance claims. The reason is simple: the same appliance can produce two very different claims depending on how the leak happened and how long it ran. Understanding the sudden-versus-gradual distinction before you file helps you present your claim accurately.

What Is the Sudden-vs-Gradual Distinction?

Most standard homeowners policies cover sudden and accidental water discharge — an event that happens unexpectedly and is discovered promptly. A supply hose that splits during a wash cycle, a drain hose that disconnects, or a door seal that fails suddenly and floods the kitchen floor all fit this pattern.

Gradual damage is the opposite: a slow seep from a worn hose connection, a drip under the dishwasher that goes unnoticed for months, or a leak a homeowner knew about but delayed fixing. Insurers generally treat long-term seepage and maintenance-related deterioration as the homeowner's responsibility, and many policies exclude it explicitly.

Which Dishwasher Leak Scenarios Are Usually Covered?

Common Scenarios at a Glance

Scenario General Treatment
Supply hose bursts during a cycle Generally sudden and accidental
Drain hose disconnects and floods Generally sudden and accidental
Slow drip unnoticed for months Often treated as gradual damage
Known leak left unrepaired Often excluded as maintenance-related

This table reflects general guidance only. Policy language varies, and Florida policies differ from one another. Your insurer's decision follows your specific policy wording, not this article.

Does Insurance Pay to Replace the Dishwasher?

Usually, no. Homeowners policies generally cover the resulting water damage to your home — floors, cabinets, drywall — but not the failed appliance itself. The dishwasher is typically considered a maintenance item. The water damage it causes is the insurable part, subject to your deductible and policy limits.

What Should I Do First After a Dishwasher Leak?

  1. Shut off water to the dishwasher at its supply valve, usually under the kitchen sink, and turn off power to the appliance.
  2. Photograph everything before cleanup: the appliance, the leak point, the water spread, and damaged flooring and cabinets.
  3. Start mitigation promptly. Insurers expect homeowners to limit further damage. Remove standing water, run fans, and keep the AC on.
  4. Call your insurer early and ask what documentation they want and whether they require an adjuster visit before repairs.
  5. Do not discard damaged materials until the insurer has seen photos or approved disposal.

How Do I Document a Dishwasher Leak Claim?

Strong documentation tells the story of a sudden event. Save the failed hose or part if a plumber replaces it, keep the plumber's report describing what failed and how, and keep a dated timeline of discovery and cleanup. If the leak was discovered during or right after a wash cycle, say so clearly — timing is what separates a sudden claim from a gradual one.
