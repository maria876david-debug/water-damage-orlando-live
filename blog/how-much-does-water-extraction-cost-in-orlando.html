How Much Does Water Extraction Cost in Orlando?

Water extraction is the first phase of water damage work, priced separately from drying and repairs. Local guides commonly cite around $3–$7 per square foot for mitigation and extraction work.

When homeowners search for extraction costs, they usually want one number. The honest answer is that extraction is only the first step, and its price is best understood as a per-square-foot range. This guide explains what that range covers, what changes it, and what it does not include — so a quote makes sense when you receive one.

What Is the Typical Per-Square-Foot Rate?

Local guides commonly cite around $3–$7 per square foot for mitigation and extraction work. That range reflects the equipment and labor to remove standing water and begin the moisture-control process across the affected area. Smaller jobs and straightforward clean-water extractions sit toward the lower end, while larger areas and contaminated water push toward the higher end.

This figure is a planning guide, not a quote. The affected area is measured during an inspection, and the final price reflects what the inspection finds.

What Does Extraction Actually Include?

  • Standing water removal with pumps and commercial-grade vacuums.
  • Initial moisture readings to map how far water spread through floors and walls.
  • Moving or lifting contents out of the wet area so extraction can reach the floor.
  • First-pass drying setup, such as positioning air movers for the drying phase that follows.

Extraction is fast work — often completed the same day — but it is not the whole job. Drying, material removal, and repairs are separate phases with their own scopes.

What Changes the Extraction Price?

  • Area affected. More square footage means more equipment time and labor.
  • Water depth and volume. Deep standing water takes longer to remove than a shallow spread.
  • Water type. Clean water is the simplest case. Contaminated water from sewage or flooding requires protective handling and disposal, which adds cost.
  • Access. Water trapped under flooring, inside wall cavities, or in hard-to-reach spaces takes more work to extract.
  • Emergency timing. After-hours emergency response can carry different pricing than a scheduled visit.

What Is NOT Included in Extraction Pricing?

Extraction pricing does not cover the drying phase, which runs over several days with dehumidifiers and air movers. It does not cover removing and replacing damaged drywall, flooring, or insulation, and it does not cover rebuilding or repainting. If a quote seems to promise the entire restoration for an extraction-range price, ask for the scope in writing — something is missing.
