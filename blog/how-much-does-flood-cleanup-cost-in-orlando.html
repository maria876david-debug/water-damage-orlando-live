How Much Does Flood Cleanup Cost in Orlando?

There is no single price for flood cleanup in Orlando. The cost is set by the affected area, water depth, contamination, and how much material must be removed and dried. Any honest price comes from an on-site inspection.

Flood cleanup is not one job. It is a sequence — extraction, drying, material removal, cleaning, and verification — and each phase has its own scope. Two Orlando homes flooded in the same storm can face very different bills, because depth, contamination, and construction differ. This guide explains the drivers so you can read a quote with confidence.

Note that this guide covers flood cleanup specifically. For the overall restoration job from start to finish, see our general Orlando water damage restoration cost guide.

What Drives Flood Cleanup Cost?

  • Area affected. More square footage means more extraction time, more drying equipment, and more labor.
  • Water depth and volume. A few inches across a garage is a different job from several feet through a ground floor.
  • Contamination level. Rainwater intrusion is the simplest case. Floodwater that has mixed with sewage, chemicals, or debris requires protective handling, disposal, and removal of porous materials that cannot be safely cleaned.
  • Materials affected. Tile and concrete usually clean up well. Drywall, insulation, carpet padding, and laminate often need removal and replacement.
  • Contents. Furniture, electronics, and stored items in the flooded area add cleaning or disposal work.
  • Access and structure. Water trapped under flooring, inside wall cavities, or in crawl spaces takes longer to reach and dry.

What Phases Make Up a Flood Cleanup?

  1. Extraction. Standing water is pumped and vacuumed out, usually the same day.
  2. Drying. Commercial air movers and dehumidifiers run for several days to pull moisture from floors, walls, and framing.
  3. Removal. Contaminated or ruined porous materials are cut out and disposed of.
  4. Cleaning. Remaining surfaces are cleaned and treated to prevent microbial growth.
  5. Verification. Moisture readings confirm the structure is dry before any rebuild begins.

Each phase is scoped separately in a proper quote. A single lump-sum number with no phase breakdown is hard to compare and easy to inflate later.

How Is Flood Cleanup Different From a Leak Cleanup?

Floodwater is almost always treated as contaminated, because it has traveled across ground, roads, and drainage systems before entering the home. That changes the work: porous materials soaked by floodwater are generally removed rather than dried and kept, protective procedures are stricter, and disposal requirements are heavier. A clean-water appliance leak, by contrast, often allows more materials to be dried and saved. This is why flood jobs cost more than similar-looking leak jobs.

Does Insurance Cover Flood Cleanup?

Standard homeowners policies generally do not cover flood damage from rising water — that is typically a separate flood insurance matter. Water damage from a burst pipe or appliance leak inside the home is a different category. If your flooding came from a storm or rising water, check whether you hold separate flood coverage and notify that carrier promptly. If it came from inside the home, your homeowners policy is the starting point. Either way, document everything before cleanup and keep all receipts.

How Do I Get an Accurate Flood Cleanup Price?

Ask for an on-site inspection and a written scope broken down by phase: extraction, drying, removal, cleaning, and verification. Ask what materials will be removed versus dried, and what the drying timeline looks like. A company that inspects first and explains each line is the one giving you a real price.
