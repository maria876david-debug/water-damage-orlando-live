Contents Restoration Questions

Can my furniture be saved after water damage?

Solid wood furniture often can be — it's dried slowly to prevent warping, then cleaned and refinished. Upholstered pieces depend on the water category: clean water damage is usually restorable, but sewage contamination means replacement. Particle board and MDF furniture swells permanently and typically can't be saved.

How are important documents restored?

Wet documents go through freeze-drying — they're frozen to halt deterioration, then vacuum-dried to remove moisture without further damage. This works for legal papers, photographs, books, and artwork. The sooner documents reach a restoration facility, the better the outcome, so don't wait.

Should I move my belongings out during restoration?

For significant water damage, yes. A pack-out service inventories, packs, and transports your contents to a secure facility for cleaning and storage while your home is restored. This protects your belongings from ongoing humidity and gives crews room to work. Everything is documented so nothing gets lost.

Does insurance cover contents cleaning?

Most homeowners policies include personal property coverage for contents damaged by covered perils. The key is documentation — photograph everything before it's moved, keep a detailed inventory, and save receipts for cleaning costs. Your adjuster will want to see what was damaged and what restoration was attempted before approving replacement.
