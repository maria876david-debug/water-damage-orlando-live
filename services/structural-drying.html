Understanding Structural Drying in Orlando

Structural Drying in Orlando involves the systematic removal of moisture from building materials and structures after water damage. This process typically employs specialized equipment like dehumidifiers and air movers to expedite drying and prevent mold growth. Our team utilizes advanced tools, including moisture meters and infrared cameras, to ensure thorough drying.

Local Orlando customers often face unique challenges due to the region's humidity and frequent rain. Whether dealing with water leaks from plumbing issues or flooding from heavy storms, timely structural drying is essential. Our affordable drying services in Orlando are designed to address these scenarios, ensuring homes remain safe and dry.

Structural Drying Questions Orlando Homeowners Ask

How long does structural drying take in Orlando?

Most Orlando homes take 2 to 5 days for complete structural drying. The timeline depends on the water category, how long materials stayed wet, and the building materials involved. Drywall dries faster than hardwood or concrete block, which are common in Central Florida construction. Daily moisture readings confirm when each area reaches safe levels.

What equipment is used for structural drying?

Professional drying uses high-velocity air movers, refrigerant or desiccant dehumidifiers, and sometimes directed heat systems. Moisture meters and thermal imaging cameras track progress inside walls and under floors. In Orlando's humid climate, commercial dehumidifiers are essential because residential units can't keep up with the moisture load.

Can I stay in my home during structural drying?

In most cases, yes. The equipment is loud but safe to be around. If the affected area is a bedroom or main living space, you may want to plan around the noise for a few days. When sewage or mold is involved, your restoration team may recommend staying elsewhere until the area is sanitized.

Why is Orlando's climate a problem for drying?

Orlando averages around 74% relative humidity, with summer months regularly above 85%. That moisture in the air slows evaporation and gives mold a head start — mold can begin growing within 24 to 48 hours on wet drywall. Professional dehumidification removes moisture from both the structure and the indoor air, which fans alone cannot do here.
