Urgent high-volume storm water pumping, mucking, and sanitizing.

Why This Happens in Orlando Homes

Orlando's flat terrain and complex lake chains, like the Butler Chain of Lakes, can overflow during intense tropical storms and hurricanes. The city's drainage infrastructure can become overwhelmed by rapid runoff, sending storm water rising into low-lying properties.

Frequently Asked Questions

Got questions? We have direct answers from IICRC certified storm damage specialists.

Talk to a storm pro
Why is storm flood water considered highly dangerous?

Storm flood water rising from streets and lawns is classified as Category 3 "black water". It carries chemical pollutants, agricultural run-off, petroleum products, and biological pathogens from overloaded sewer systems, making it highly hazardous to touch or breathe without protective gear.

What is a "flood cut" and when is it necessary?

A flood cut is a neat horizontal line cut into your drywall twelve to eighteen inches above the highest water level. This allows us to remove the heavily contaminated plaster and wet insulation behind it, exposing the wall studs so we can clean, sanitize, and dry them.

How do you protect a flooded home from mold growth?

We apply professional broad-spectrum sanitizers to kill active mold spores immediately. We then run high-efficiency LGR dehumidifiers and HEPA air scrubbers to reduce the indoor relative humidity to below forty-five percent, which naturally stops mold from cultivating.

Does homeowners insurance cover storm flood damage?

Standard homeowners policies generally exclude damage caused by rising ground water or surface flooding. You typically need a separate flood insurance policy from the National Flood Insurance Program (NFIP) or a private insurer to cover storm-related rising water.
