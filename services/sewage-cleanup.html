Sewage Cleanup Questions Orlando Homeowners Ask

Is sewage backup dangerous to clean up myself?

Yes. Sewage is classified as Category 3 black water and can carry E. coli, salmonella, hepatitis A, and parasites. Without proper protective equipment and disinfection protocols, you risk serious illness. Florida health guidelines recommend professional remediation for any sewage intrusion beyond a minor toilet overflow caught immediately.

What causes sewage backups in Orlando homes?

The most common causes are tree root intrusion into older clay or cast-iron sewer lines, heavy rain overwhelming municipal systems during hurricane season, grease buildup in kitchen lines, and aging infrastructure in neighborhoods like College Park and Winter Park. Orlando's high water table can also push groundwater into compromised pipes.

Will my homeowners insurance cover sewage cleanup?

Standard homeowners policies typically exclude sewer backup unless you carry a specific sewer backup endorsement. Check your policy for this rider — it usually costs little to add but covers thousands in cleanup costs. Document everything with photos before cleanup begins, as insurers require proof of the damage extent.

How do you sanitize after sewage removal?

After removing contaminated water and unsalvageable materials, all affected surfaces get treated with EPA-registered antimicrobial agents. Porous materials like drywall, carpet padding, and insulation are removed rather than cleaned, because contaminants penetrate too deeply. Hard surfaces are scrubbed, disinfected, and verified with ATP testing where appropriate.
