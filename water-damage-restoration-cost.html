How much does water damage restoration cost per square foot?

Restoration pricing starts with the category of water, because the category decides the safety protocol, the equipment, and how much material can be dried in place versus removed. The industry sorts water into three categories under the ANSI/IICRC S500 standard. Clean water costs the least to fix. Black water can cost more than twice as much per square foot, because everything porous it touches has to come out.

Water Category Typical Sources Angi Average Cost Per Sq. Ft. HomeGuide Extraction & Drying Cost Per Sq. Ft.
Category 1 — Clean Water Broken supply line, faucet leak, rainwater intrusion, tub overflow $3.50 $4 – $6
Category 2 — Gray Water Dishwasher or washing machine overflow, toilet bowl overflow without feces, sump pump failure $5.25 $6 – $9
Category 3 — Black Water Sewage backup, toilet overflow with feces, storm or river flooding, groundwater intrusion $7.50 $9 – $12

All figures are U.S. national data retrieved November 13, 2026. Why two sets of numbers? Angi's per-square-foot figures are averages across completed repair projects. HomeGuide's figures cover the extraction and drying stage specifically. A real invoice usually contains both stages — mitigation first, repairs after — which is why a full job can land above the per-square-foot rate for either stage alone. One more Orlando factor: clean water does not stay clean here. In Florida heat, Category 1 water left standing degrades toward Category 2 conditions within roughly 24 to 48 hours as bacteria multiply — which is why the same leak costs more on day three than it did on day one.

What does a typical Orlando restoration job cost in total?

Per-square-foot rates explain small jobs. For whole-job budgeting, published totals and damage classes are more useful. Angi's 2026 data puts the national average water damage restoration cost at $3,874, with most homeowners paying between $1,385 and $6,381 and full projects ranging from $450 to $16,000. HomeGuide puts a typical job at $2,000 to $6,000 and prices by damage class:

Damage Class What It Looks Like Average Repair Price (HomeGuide)
Class 1 — Minimal Small leak in one part of a room, limited saturation $150 – $500
Class 2 — Significant Up to 12 inches of standing water in at least one room $1,500 – $5,000
Class 3 — Extensive Water has permeated one or more rooms — ceilings, walls, flooring, and subfloor $5,000 – $15,000
Class 4 — Disaster Severe damage throughout the home from prolonged flooding or a hurricane $15,000 – $100,000

Simple worked examples, using the published rates above (this is arithmetic on national figures, not a quote): a 200-square-foot living room soaked by a clean supply-line break prices out around $700 at Angi's $3.50 average, or $800 to $1,200 at HomeGuide's extraction-and-drying range. A 500-square-foot washing machine overflow — Category 2 gray water — runs roughly $3,000 to $4,500 for extraction and drying alone. The same 500 square feet hit by a sewage backup prices at $4,500 to $6,000 before any drywall, flooring, or cabinet replacement is added. Repairs after drying are separate line items: HomeGuide lists replacement drywall at $1.50 to $3.50 per square foot and mold remediation, when growth has started, at $15 to $30 per square foot. Fast emergency water extraction is what keeps a job in the lower classes — water pulled out within hours often dries in place, while water left for days turns into demolition and rebuild.

What drives the cost of water damage restoration up or down?

The water category

This is the single biggest pricing factor. Category 1 water can usually be extracted and dried with standard equipment. Category 2 adds antimicrobial treatment and closer scrutiny of soft materials. Category 3 requires full protective equipment, containment, and disposal of every porous material the water touched — drywall, carpet, padding, insulation, and upholstered furniture — which is why its per-square-foot price is the highest of the three.

How much area is wet — including the area you cannot see

Crews price the moisture map, not the puddle. Water wicks up drywall, travels under tile and laminate, and saturates concrete block cavities — all common in Central Florida construction. Structural drying with thermal imaging and moisture meters finds that hidden spread before pricing is finalized, which is why a quote can legitimately grow after the first inspection if the map shows more wet structure than the visible damage suggested.

How long the water sat

Every day of delay moves a job up a class. Mold can begin growing on wet drywall and carpet padding within 24 to 48 hours in Orlando's humidity, and once remediation is added to mitigation, the invoice changes category entirely. After-hours and weekend emergencies can also carry added cost — but waiting until Monday to save it usually costs more than the surcharge.

What has to be removed and rebuilt

Mitigation — extraction, drying, sanitizing — is only the first invoice. Flood cuts, drywall replacement, flooring replacement, cabinet work, and repainting are billed on top, priced per material. Jobs where materials dry in place are the cheapest jobs in every published dataset.

Whether mold has already started

If the 24-to-48-hour window was missed, mold remediation becomes its own scoped project with containment and HEPA air scrubbing. Angi budgets mold removal as an additional $1,200 to $3,800 on a typical restoration plan; HomeGuide's per-square-foot remediation range is $15 to $30. How much does odor removal cost after water damage in Orlando?

A musty or sour smell that survives the drying equipment is one of the most common reasons Orlando homeowners call us — and it is almost never just a smell. Odor is a symptom: it means moisture or contaminated material is still inside the structure, feeding the bacteria and mold that produce the gases you are smelling. That is why professional odor removal is priced and performed as the last stage of restoration, not the first.

Why does the smell linger after the water is gone?

Porous materials — drywall, carpet padding, insulation, wood framing — absorb odor compounds while they are wet and keep releasing them long after surfaces feel dry. Wall cavities, subfloors, ceiling voids, and HVAC drip pans stay wet the longest because air does not move through them, so they become hidden odor reservoirs. In Orlando the cycle is worse: a wall cavity sitting above 60% relative humidity can rewet material that felt dry to the touch, which is why a smell often fades in dry weather and returns when summer humidity climbs. Each smell points at a source: musty or earthy usually means mold or mildew in damp material; sour usually means bacteria in wet carpet, padding, or an AC drip pan; rotten-egg or sewage smells mean contaminated water or sewer gas and need action the same day.

How do professionals actually remove the odor?

In a fixed order: remove, dry, clean, then deodorize. First, unsalvageable material comes out — soaked carpet pad, wet insulation, and saturated drywall cannot be deodorized in place. Second, the structure is dried to verified moisture readings, not to a dry feel. Third, hard surfaces are scrubbed and treated with antimicrobials where the water was contaminated. Only then do deodorizing treatments work: HEPA and activated-carbon air scrubbers filter particles and adsorb odor gases during the job; hydroxyl generators break down odor molecules over several days and can run in occupied spaces; thermal fogging drives deodorizer into cracks and porous surfaces in an emptied space; and ozone — used only in sealed, unoccupied rooms, because the U.S. EPA cautions that ozone is a lung irritant and does not effectively remove mold at levels safe to breathe — oxidizes heavy residual odor after the source is gone. Residual odor in cleaned, dry framing can be locked in with an odor-blocking sealer. Any contractor whose whole plan is to fog or spray a room without removing the source is treating the symptom, and the smell will be back within days.

What does odor removal cost?

Professional water damage odor removal typically costs $200 to $3,000, according to RestoreAdvisor's pricing research (retrieved November 13, 2026), with most of the spread explained by one question: does odor-soaked material have to be removed as part of the job? Deodorizing a space that has already been extracted, dried, and cleaned sits at the low end. Jobs that add carpet, pad, drywall, or subfloor removal climb toward the high end. Within a job, surface cleaning and antimicrobial treatment is typically a few hundred dollars, while HEPA and carbon air scrubbers are commonly billed per day of operation. Odor treatment is normally a small part of a total restoration bill — extraction, drying, and rebuilding are what drive the total — and when the odor traces back to a covered sudden water loss, deodorization is usually scoped inside the same claim. How much does burst pipe water cleanup cost?

A burst supply line is the most common Category 1 emergency in Orlando homes — and one of the cheapest to clean up if it is caught fast, because the water starts out clean. Two separate costs apply. The plumbing repair itself: HomeGuide puts burst pipe repair at $150 to $250 per linear foot of pipe replaced. The water damage around it: Angi puts repairing a burst pipe and its associated damage at $1,000 to $4,000 in total, with the spread driven by how far the water traveled before the main valve was shut off.

The cleanup side is priced exactly like the table at the top of this page — by category and square footage. A burst pipe caught within the hour, with water confined to one tiled room, is a small extraction-and-drying job. The same break running all weekend soaks drywall, baseboards, and subfloor across several rooms, and the invoice follows the moisture map. Our dedicated burst pipe water damage cleanup page walks through the response step by step, and the first 24 hours after a burst pipe guide covers what to do before the crew arrives: shut off the main valve, kill power to wet areas, photograph everything for your insurer, and do not throw damaged items away until your adjuster has seen them.

Water Damage Restoration Cost Questions Orlando Homeowners Ask

Why do two companies quote such different prices for the same water damage?

Usually because they scoped different jobs. One quote may cover extraction and drying only; the other may include demolition, mold remediation, and rebuild. Ask each contractor which water category they recorded, how many square feet their moisture map shows, and which stages are inside the number. The category line alone can double a per-square-foot rate — and the category is set by the water's source, not by how the water looks.

Is the cheapest part of the job the water removal itself?

Often, yes. Extraction and drying for a contained clean-water job can be a few hundred to a few thousand dollars. The expensive parts of water damage are what come after slow responses: Category 3 protocols, mold remediation, and reconstruction. That is the honest math behind every "call fast" message on this site — speed is the one discount that is always available.

Does homeowners insurance cover water damage restoration in Florida?

Standard Florida policies generally cover sudden, accidental discharges — a burst supply line, a failed water heater, an appliance line break — after your deductible. Long-term seepage over weeks is frequently excluded as a maintenance issue, rising flood water requires separate flood coverage, and many modern Florida policies carry specific caps on non-weather water damage and mold sub-limits. Contractors in the network document moisture logs and itemized billing your adjuster can work from, but this service does not take over your claim or act as a public adjuster.
